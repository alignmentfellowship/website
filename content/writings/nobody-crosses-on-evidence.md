## I. The Corner

For a while, some years ago, I stood on sidewalks in Portland holding up a screen. When work took
me to New York, and to London, I stood on sidewalks there too.

The screen played footage from inside factory farms. My job, and the job of the people standing
with me, was to hold it where people were walking, and then to invite them into a conversation about what
they had just seen.

Few accepted the invitation.

What I was doing was sincere, and everything after this depends on that being true. The suffering on that screen is real. Nothing here walks that back, and if any
sentence in this essay reads as a man distancing himself from a cause in order to make a point
about himself, I have written it badly. The goal was to end suffering. It is still the goal.

What I had wrong was not the horror. It was everything else.

At the time I had an explanation for the people who kept walking, and it came so easily that it never presented itself to me as a theory. *They don't want to know.* Say that sentence out
loud and listen to what it does. In five words it sorts a public sidewalk into two kinds of person — the ones who will look and the ones who won't — and it files the man saying it on the
better side, unprompted, free of charge. I did not experience this as arrogance. I experienced it
as grief. That is the part I'd like other people to understand, and it's the part that makes
the whole thing worth writing down: **the sorting did not feel like judgment. It felt like caring
more.**

And the strange thing, the thing I could not make sense of for years afterward, is that the
argument was airtight. That was the whole reason I was standing there. I was not asking anyone to
accept a doctrine or trust my character or join anything. I was asking them to look at a recording
of a thing that was happening, at that moment, while they were buying lunch. There
was no counter-evidence. There has never been any counter-evidence. I was holding as strong a hand as anybody gets dealt in a public argument, and I was losing it, over and over, to
people who simply kept walking. Three cities, two continents, one result.

For a long time I believed the loss was a failure of nerve — mine, for not being persuasive enough,
or theirs, for not being brave enough. Both of those explanations have the same shape. Both of them
say the crossing did not happen because somebody was not *good* enough at it.

Neither of them is true. **Nobody crosses on evidence.**

The footage was not weak. The people walking past were not short of information. What was between
us was not a shortage of anything, and it could not have been closed by a better screen, a better
argument, or a better man holding it. Something else was there — something with a shape, which I
could feel every time we set up and could not name — and I have been trying to name it ever since.

It starts a long way from a sidewalk.

## II. From the Top to the Bottom

At the east of the garden, after the expulsion, God sets a guard. The King James is unusually
specific about what the guard is for: cherubim, and a flaming sword which turned every way, *to
keep the way of the tree of life.*[^eden]

Notice the object of that sentence. What is guarded is not the tree. It is **the way**.

Hold that, because the Greek translation the first Christians read renders that Hebrew word with
the one that turns up in the mouth of a man about to be killed for saying things like it: *I am the
way.*[^way] And on the afternoon He is killed, the curtain in the temple — woven, the instructions
in Exodus say, with cherubim, the same figures set at the same distance for the same
purpose[^cherubim] — is torn *in twain from the top to the bottom.*[^rent] From the top. From the
God-ward side, which is the reading [*The Way Home Is
Down*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-way-home-is-down) makes at length. The sword was not sheathed. It was passed through.

Submitting a will to God is older than the cross. Abraham's was given over, and Job's, and the
psalms are full of people doing it. What could not happen was the coming
home. The way was guarded. Submission was available and **return was not.** And what came through the guard was not a
submitted will. It was a man for whom the separation itself had ended — a larger thing than
obedience, and what following Him finally means, and the subject of another essay. The letter to the
Hebrews names the curtain and the body in one breath: there is now boldness to enter *by a new and
living way, which He hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, His flesh.*[^heb]
The road runs through the place where the separation stopped.

So the tear is not where the choice began. It is where the yes acquired somewhere to arrive.

**What opened was a way.** Not a place. Not a territory anyone was moved into, not a door that
swung and left a visible gap in the air over Jerusalem. A road. And a road has a property that a place doesn't have, which is that **it is nothing at all to a person who is not walking on it.**
Lay a road across a country and the country looks, to anybody standing still in it, exactly as it
did the day before. The grass is the same. The weather is the same. Everyone is where they were.

Which is the first honest answer to the obvious question. If something genuinely
happened on that Friday — if the guard came down, if the way home stopped being barred — then why
does the world look like this? Why is the news what it is? Where is the difference?

The difference is not in the world. **It is in who is walking.**

That gives us two stories running through one set of streets. Not two worlds; there's one world, and everyone in it is buying lunch on the same afternoon. Two stories about what the afternoon is,
and what a person is, and whose the day belongs to — and for a very long time those two stories
have been near enough identical from outside that nobody could tell, by looking at a street, which
one the people on it were living in.

That's the situation. The rest of this is an attempt to say what the two stories actually are, why
they cannot stay indistinguishable, and what happens as they pull apart.[^apoc]

## III. Two Wills

**The two stories are two wills.** That is the whole argument in one line.

There is a will that defends itself. And there is a will that seeks the LORD's will, out of love.

Everything else — the two ways of reading a sidewalk, the two accounts of what a day is for — comes off that one distinction and nothing else. The stories are not made of different
events. The people in them are not in different places and are not headed for different weather
next Tuesday; they are running on different wills, and a will is invisible.

Take the will that defends itself first, since it's the one I know from the inside. It is not
wicked and it is not stupid. It is a will that experiences itself as a separate thing with a
boundary, in a world of other separate things, and it does what any such thing would do: it holds
its position, it keeps its accounts, and it decides. It decides constantly. It rates what happens
to it, sorts what it meets into what serves it and what threatens it, and issues findings. That last habit is the **gavel**, and I'm taking the image from [*The Way Home Is Down*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-way-home-is-down), which makes the case for it.[^gavel] The short version is that
adjudicating the world is not an occasional vice of a separate self. It is what a separate self
is *for*. Take the judging away and the thing has no function left.

The other will wants what God wants, because it loves Them. That is the whole of it. It is not a
better-informed will; it does not know the plan, it has not been shown the route, and it has no
more access to the shape of the world than I had on that sidewalk. What it has is a different
answer to a single question — *whose day is this* — and the answer changes what it does with every
hour in it, without changing one visible fact about the hour. And even that is the door and not the
room; what is on the far side of a will given over is not this essay's to argue.

And that answer is not a sentence anybody has to be able to say. I have argued at length in [*The
Highest Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak) that the criterion is the
direction of your feet and not your vocabulary, and the scene the tradition itself uses for the
sorting turns on it: the ones who are welcomed are *confused*, and ask when they ever saw Him hungry.[^sheep] **Neither group knows.** So the second will is not the will of the people who have the
word *God* in their mouths. Some of them have it and are running the first one; some who have never used it have set the gavel down and spend their days on somebody else's trouble. What
is being described here is a posture, and postures are legible in what a person does with an
afternoon, which is exactly why the two stories look alike from a street corner.

The word the tradition uses for the change from the first to the second is *metanoia*, and our
translations render it *repentance*, which sends English speakers somewhere the thing itself does
not go — toward remorse, toward having felt sufficiently bad about it. The prophets are not asking
for a feeling; they are asking for a **turn**: *turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways.*[^turn] And
where the change is shown rather than named, it is shown as exactly that. The younger son in the far
country does not first feel terrible; he **comes to himself**, and then he gets up and
goes.[^prodigal] The mind changes and the feet follow, and neither of those is a journey to a
different country — he was in the far country the whole time, and the turn happened where he
stood.

## IV. The Kingdom Is Not Somewhere

**The kingdom is not a place.** It is where God's will is done — not a poetic gloss but the plain
sense of the prayer, which is why the prayer puts the two in apposition: *Thy kingdom come. Thy will
be done in earth, as it is in heaven.*[^prayer] The second line is not a further request. It is what
the first one meant.

If it isn't a place, there is nowhere to steer to — which is why this was never a fork you get to
make from above.

You cannot pick the better one. There is no selecting it, no aligning to it, no technique for
arriving in the version of your life where it worked out; a sibling essay,
[*Where the Timelines Agree*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/where-the-timelines-agree), argues at length that there is no such steering. **The fork is between
wills**, it is available in the next five seconds, and it has to
be taken again after that.

And the prayer's apposition tells you what it would mean to be unable to abide there. A will that has not been given over
does not get turned away at a gate; there is no gate, and the doorman you were worried about does
not exist — which is [*The Highest Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak)'s sentence. It is worse and simpler than exclusion. A will whose entire
function is to adjudicate has, in a country where the adjudicating is not its to do, **nothing to
do**. Not nothing permitted. Nothing to *do*. That is the sense in which good and evil, as we
know them, are not operating there: not that cruelty and kindness have stopped differing, but that
the rating of what God wills was never our office, and a self that exists to hold the office
cannot stay in a room where the office does not exist.

Which is not the same as being kept out, and the difference matters at the end of this essay. The
way is open to everyone; nothing was withheld and nobody was screened. **A person can be standing
on the road and not walking it**, and that's the ordinary condition, and it is mine on most days.

One boundary on that, and it is not a small one. **This is a claim about adjudicating God, not
about telling right from wrong.** The difference between a kind act and a cruel one is real and is
worth a life, and an essay that blurred it would have made itself into a muzzle.

## V. Everything It Builds

**In the story of separation the independent will has apparent free rein, and everything it builds
carries the seed of its own destruction.**

Take a fence, because it is the plainest thing a defended will makes, and because what is true of
it is true of the rest.

A fence is an answer to a question about what is outside it. It does not discover the answer — it
asserts one, and from then on the answer has to go on being true, and keeping it true is somebody's
job every year from now on. Nobody has ever built a fence and been finished.

Start from what the will is. A will that defends itself requires something to defend against. That
is not a psychological observation; it is what the words mean. Defense is a two-place relation, and
if you subtract the second term the first one has no content. So a self constituted by defending is
a self that **cannot be at rest in the absence of a threat** — and where it does not find one, it
will find one anyway, because the alternative is not peace, it is the dissolution of the only thing
it knows how to be.

Watch what that does to its works, and notice first that most of them look nothing like fences. It
builds hospitals, and raises children, and writes music that will outlast every border anybody ever
drew. **But everything it builds, it builds for the defense** — and a thing built for the defense is
a fence whatever else it also is. The hospital, the child, the music, the reputation, the case, the
account of who did what to whom: each of them is marking a line, and each of them has to go on being
held. **And the line has to be held against something the line itself defined.** The threat is not
discovered by the defense; it is constituted by it. So the cost never ends and it never falls,
because nothing inside the arrangement can ever discharge it — there is no state of the world in
which the defended will is finished defending. That is the seed, and it goes in at the founding,
and it goes into the good things worst of all, which is why they are the hardest to set down.

And the cost comes out of the thing being defended. You keep the reputation by spending the
friendships it was for. You hold the border by spending the country behind it. You keep the position
by spending the work it was supposed to make possible. So nothing has to
defeat the arrangement. It consumes what it was built to protect, and that is a destruction rather
than a decline.

None of that is new here. [*The
Knowledge of Good and Evil*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-knowledge-of-good-and-evil) argues
that what was taken at the tree was a *worldview* — the conviction that reality is split into two
powers with a referee's seat between them — and that the split is the wound. That essay makes the
case at the level of **knowledge**: seeing double. I'm making it one layer down, at the level of
**will**: not seeing two, but *defending against* the second one you see. The two are the same
disease described at different depths, and the second is where the destruction actually enters,
because a mistaken picture does not build anything and a defended will builds constantly.

**I am not telling you which century you are in.** The claim above is structural. It was true of
every empire that ever congratulated itself on its defenses, and it is true this afternoon, and it
will be true of arrangements nobody has thought of yet. It has no date in it, no schedule, no signs
to read off the news, and I'm not going to give it any. A great deal of writing that sounds like
this one gets its energy from an implied clock, and the clock is always the tell — it is the
separate will doing the one thing it does, promoted to cosmology.

And nothing here is a sentence being handed down. The freedom to build like that is real freedom, and
it is not being withdrawn; the road out of the garden was a genuine road, and I've argued
alongside [*The Freedom to Walk Away*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-freedom-to-walk-away)
that a love which cannot be refused is not love. *Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also
reap*[^sow] is not a threat of retaliation. It is a description of terrain — the ground you end up
standing on is the ground your steps took you to — and [*The Highest
Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak) says so at length. The independent will
is not being punished. It is being permitted, exactly as advertised, and permission is the whole
of what it asked for.

## VI. The Gulf

Two stories, one street, no visible difference. That was §II's answer, and it is only the first
half of the truth, because **the two do not stay indistinguishable.**

Nothing could keep them there. A will expresses itself in what it does, and what it does accumulates. Give a defended
will and a given-over will the same street and the same century, and for a long while you will see
two people who look about the same — both working, both tired, both fond of their dogs. Give them
long enough and you get two different sets of institutions, two different accounts of what a person
is, two different things done with a stranger. The divergence starts as interpretation, which is
invisible. It does not stay there.

And as it widens, something opens between the two that is worse than disagreement.

**From inside the story of separation, handing your will
over is not humility. It is insanity.** Look at it from there honestly — I can, because I lived
there, and a good deal of me has never moved out — and the proposal is that you should stop adjudicating a
world that is visibly on fire, surrender the only instrument you have ever had for protecting
anything, and do so on the authority of Someone you cannot produce. There is no version of that
which looks responsible. It looks like a man dropping his end of the rope.

And from the other side the reverse is just as stark. Watching a will grip its gavel harder while what it built with the gavel comes apart in its hands does not read as prudence either.

So the gulf. And now the thing I could not have told you on that sidewalk.

**Nobody crosses on evidence.**

Not because the evidence is bad. Because the story of separation is a **closed evidentiary system**.
A will that defends itself is not a neutral instrument that happens to hold a mistaken view; it is a
generator of confirmations. Everything it meets, it meets as a claim to be adjudicated, and it
adjudicates from where it stands, and the finding comes back the way findings do. Show it a horror
and it will file the horror correctly and reach for the gavel. Show it a kindness and it will file
that too. **The evidence is not
weak on this side of the gulf. It is all on this side of the gulf**, and there is no quantity of it
that adds up to a crossing, because the crossing is not the conclusion of an argument. It is a
change in who is arguing.

That looks like a contradiction of something I have already published. [*I Believe in
You*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/i-believe-in-you) argues that faith is **unseen, not
unevidenced** — that Hebrews is describing something you cannot see rather than something you have
no reason for, and that the two get confused constantly to the tradition's cost. I still think
that's right. The claim here is not its opposite. What is unavailable at the gulf is evidence *of
the kind the defended will accepts*, which is the kind that arrives as a finding and settles a
case. The crossing is the other thing that essay named: belief-**in** rather than belief-**that**,
which is a vow and not a verdict. You do not conclude your way into it. You say it, and then you
are the one who said it.

Which puts the obvious question to this essay. *You have just argued that argument cannot cross the gulf. What,
precisely, do you think you are doing?*

Fair. And the answer is not that this one is special. **I'm not trying to walk anybody across.** I
don't know how. What an essay can do is smaller: describe the shape of the thing, so that the person who is already unclenching has a
word for what is happening to them, and so that the person doing what I was doing on that sidewalk
might stop mistaking a gulf for a failure of nerve. **Nobody was ever argued across.** I wasn't. Jesus
found me, and I could not reconstruct for you the argument that did it, because there wasn't
one.

Which is what I was doing wrong on the corner, and it took me years and a different kind of work to see it.

I was standing on my side of a gulf, holding out reasons.

I had the reasons. I have never stopped having them. And I was offering them to people in the one
currency that cannot buy a crossing, and calling the failure of the transaction a fact about their
character.

So I stopped, and went and did something else. I volunteered at Odd Man Inn, a farmed-animal sanctuary in
Washougal — a place where the most-used animals in the world are simply allowed to live out their
lives. I built
shelters. I raised fences — the other kind, the kind built so that something can be left alone
inside it. I shoveled pig poop, which is more of the work than anyone tells you. Nobody was persuaded of anything at a fence post. Nobody walked past and was
converted. It was, by the only metric I had been using on that sidewalk, a total collapse in reach.

**Focusing on the solution instead of the problem made all the difference.**

I don't fully know why yet. But the shape of it is clear enough to name. On the corner I was fighting the vision I didn't want, which required a them,
which required a fence, which required me to keep the gavel in my hand in order to be of any use at
all. At the sanctuary I was building the thing I did want, which required nothing from anyone
except the next fence post. The goal never changed. **The will did**, and everything else moved.

## VII. Why It Stops Looking Reasonable

The ask this tradition makes is a hard one, and it is a *reasonable* one. Set down the independent
will. Stop rating the world. Trust the One who is actually holding the accounts. That is difficult
in the way a real change is difficult, and ordinary people have managed it in kitchens and prisons
and hospital corridors without any special equipment.

Now put the same person in open water at night, holding a life preserver.

Ask them to let go of it and you're not making a philosophical proposal. Whatever you can see about
the depth of the water, they can't; what they have is the thing in their hands and the fact that
they are still up. And the colder it gets, the more obviously right it is to hold on. Nobody in
that water has ever been talked out of it by someone standing on a dock.

**The apparent evils in the story of separation do not merely make it harder to put the gavel down;
they make holding it feel obligatory.**

A person watching something monstrous is not being asked, in that moment, to give up a petty habit
of criticism; they're being asked to lay down what feels like the last instrument of justice in a
world that has clearly run out of them. And the worse it gets, the more the instrument seems
required — which is the whole difficulty, and it doesn't run on the century. It runs on how much of
the horror a person has let themselves see. Every atrocity is genuine evidence that *someone must judge* — and there is no
argument you can make to a person in that state that does not sound, correctly, like a request that
they stop caring.

So the story defends itself with the very thing that proves it needs judging. The grip tightens on
the way down. And the tighter it gets, the further the two stories part, which makes the crossing
look more insane, which tightens the grip again. **The trench seals itself, and it seals hardest in
exactly the people who are most awake to how bad it is.**

That is not a diagnosis of anyone else. I dug that trench. I dug it while holding a screen, and the depth of it
was in direct proportion to how much I had let myself look.

**I cannot use the state of the world as evidence for any of this.** [*Where the Timelines Agree*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/where-the-timelines-agree) establishes why: the badness of a moment is not evidence about the route, in either direction, and
a slice of a thing reports nothing about the whole of it. So this is a mechanism and not a reading
of the times. I am not telling you the world is worse than it was. I don't know whether it is. What
a defended will does with a horror is what it has always done with one, and the response is
self-sealing.

## VIII. Three Ways This Becomes Cruel

Three things can be done with this essay that would make it worth not having written.

**(a) "Good — then I'm on the right one."**

This is the one the structure hands you free of charge, and it is the dangerous one, because it
does not feel like a verdict on anybody. It feels like relief.

There is no instrument for auditing your own will, and the moment a person has concluded they are
on the good story, the thing that reached the conclusion was **the first will**, doing the only
thing it knows how to do — rating, sorting, issuing a finding, and this time filing itself on the
better side. That is the move from §I with a cosmology bolted on, which is the worst upgrade it has
ever received. **This piece does not come with a chair to sort a sidewalk from.** I had one of
those. It is exactly what failed.

Which disposes of its second form too — *so the people still gripping the gavel are the
destruction.* I was the one dug into a camp, and not as a young man's phase I have grown out of;
the same reflex is available to me this afternoon and gets taken more often than I would like. A
count of camps was never a moral theory. There are not two kinds of person. There are two wills,
and both of them have been in me today.

**(b) "So stop fighting injustice."**

No, and this is where the boundary from §IV gets its teeth. **Discernment reads an act; the gavel
sentences a person.** That distinction is [*The Way Home Is
Down*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-way-home-is-down)'s and it is made properly there:
naming a cruelty as cruelty is what love requires, and refusing to file the human being who did it
onto the far side of a line marked *evil* is a different operation entirely. You can do the first
with total seriousness and never do the second. The test that essay put on the table is one I'll
take on this one: **any use of these ideas that makes it easier to hurry a survivor, to hush a
victim, or to spare a perpetrator the naming of what he did has inverted the argument into its
enemy.** If a reader can turn this essay into a muzzle, I've written it badly, and I would rather
know.

And the biography refuses the quietist reading without my help. The sanctuary was not withdrawal
from the fight. It was fences, in the morning, in the rain, for animals nobody else was going to take. If
the alternative to the gavel were doing less, I would not be making this argument, because I have
done both and one of them involves considerably more shoveling.

**(c) "So the suffering is the world getting what it had coming."**

The inference I have refused before, and it does not get in through the back door because the
subject is large. *Deviation causes suffering* does not entail *suffering implies
deviation* — one is a claim about a road, the other is a verdict on a person, and the second is not
contained in the first. [*The Highest
Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak) makes that case at length, and points out
that the two places our own scripture puts the inference in someone's mouth are the two places it
is condemned: Job's friends, who are told *ye have not spoken of Me the thing that is
right*,[^job] and the disciples asking who sinned that a man was born blind, who are answered in
one word before they are answered in any others. *Neither.*[^neither]

Scale it up and it gets worse rather than better. Nothing here licenses reading a catastrophe, a
nation, or a year as a verdict on the people inside it. That is the oldest false comfort there is,
and an essay about the end of a story is precisely where somebody would go looking for it.

## IX. Yoda

**There are not two kinds of people.**

[*The Highest Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak) ended by refusing to leave you with a mountaintop.
[*Where the Timelines Agree*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/where-the-timelines-agree) ended by refusing to leave you with a coordinate. Two wills was a way of pointing. If what you are carrying out of this is a picture of two kinds of people, or two roads
with the better sort walking one of them, I have handed you a cleaner map to the wrong country.
There is one world, and one road, and a will that is given over or is not — in the same person, on the same afternoon, more than once.

What survives is small enough to carry and old enough that I did not find it.

*Thy will be done* is not a request, and it is not resignation dressed for church. It is the thing
itself — the sentence by which a will stops being its own. You don't say it to learn what happens
next. You say it to do it, and then it is done, and then in about four minutes you will need to say
it again. It's available in a hospital corridor and on a sidewalk and at a fence post. It was
available while I was standing there with the screen, and I would not have understood the offer.

At the sanctuary I fell in love with a pig named Yoda.

Yoda was small, and scrappy, and he never backed down from a fight.

I have thought about him for years afterward, because of the one
thing about him I could not fix and could not explain to him: **he was already inside the fence** — on land where nothing was coming for him, fed by people whose whole purpose was that he
should get to live out his life — and he was still a small animal who would not back down from a fight.

I don't know what he thought he was braced for. I never will, and it isn't mine to say. What I
know is that there was no way to tell him, and that this was not a limitation of his intelligence
but of the situation: the only thing that could have reached him was time, and being fed, and the
fight never arriving, over and over, until the readiness had nothing left to hold up.

And he was loved the whole time. Whatever he believed about his position did not slow that down at
all.

That is what I have instead of a plan, and it is not much of one. People I
love are dug into the trench, gripping the gavel harder as the reasons to grip it multiply, and the
better I understand the mechanism the less it tells me about how to reach anyone inside it. I know
what does not work, because I did it on sidewalks in three cities with an unanswerable argument in my hands. Nobody crosses on evidence, and I had the evidence. **It is tragic, and I do not know how to help them.**

One thing I have seen. Rock bottom has humbled a great many people who could not be reached any other
way; that is the whole testimony of the rooms, and [*The Sheep in the
Basement*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-sheep-in-the-basement) is where I have written
about what happens there. My hope is that the same is true one size larger — that the separate self,
clinging to its particular story of separation the way a man clings to a life preserver in water
he could stand up in, comes to the end of what clinging can do, and unclenches,
and turns.

A hope like that sits one inch from wishing the bottom on somebody, and anyone who reads this and
starts looking forward to a reckoning has ended up back on the corner with the screen. Nobody has ever been improved by being
right about that.

I don't know how far off any of this is, or whether *off* is even the right word for something that
is not a schedule. I know the way was opened from the other side, and that it is a way and not a
place, and that the only motion available on it is the next step, and that the step is not a
technique.

And the fight you have never backed down from — the one you have needed, the one that has kept
you alive, the one you would be a fool to set down while the world looks like this —

nobody is coming.

You are already inside the fence.

[^eden]: Genesis 3:24, KJV: *"So **[They]** drove out the man; and **[They]** placed at the east of
the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the
tree of life."* The King James reads *he* in both places. The subject is the Father, and this
publication's pronoun for the Father is They/Them, so the substitution is made **in brackets** —
the bracket marking the word as mine rather than the text's, since an unmarked pronoun inside a
quotation reads as the original and lets an inherited grammatical accident pass for revelation.

[^way]: John 14:6 — and the verse before it is Thomas asking *how can we know the way?*, so the
answer arrives as a reply to a question about a road. The bridge between the two is the Septuagint, which renders the Hebrew *derek*
of Genesis 3:24 with *hodos* — the word John's gospel uses. In the King James they are simply the
same English word, which is a fact about the translation and not about either original; the Greek
Old Testament is what makes the connection a real one rather than an artifact, and the body says so
rather than resting on the English.

[^heb]: Hebrews 10:19–20, KJV: *"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by
the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way, which He hath consecrated for us, through the veil,
that is to say, His flesh."* The deity pronouns here are the Son's and already match house practice,
so no bracket is needed. The letter is drawing the same line drawn above — the curtain, and
a way through it — and *that is to say, His flesh* is its own gloss, not mine: the text identifies
the curtain with the body. What that identification means for what He was — and for what
following Him asks past obedience — is the argument of a separate essay, and is only pointed at
here.

[^cherubim]: Exodus 26:31 — the veil *"of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen of
cunning work: with cherubims shall it be made."* The connection between the woven cherubim and the
cherubim at Eden's gate is an interpretive one, made at length in [*The Way Home Is Down*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-way-home-is-down).

[^rent]: Matthew 27:51; the parallel is Mark 15:38. The texts specify *from the top to the bottom*;
reading that as *from the God-ward side* is interpretive, and is offered as such — again, that
essay's argument and not this one's.

[^apoc]: The Greek *apokalypsis* is an uncovering — the removal of something that was over a thing.
It is the title of the last book of the New Testament and the root of the English *apocalypse*,
which has traveled a long way from it. I mention the derivation because a torn curtain is a
strikingly literal instance of it, and I am leaning on it for nothing: what a word was modeled on
is not what a sentence means, and no claim in this essay rests on the etymology. Nothing here draws
on Revelation's imagery or its sequence, which belong to a different argument.

[^gavel]: The image reads the fall as the taking of a *seat* — a verdict seized that was never
ours — rather than as the breaking of a rule. Borrowed here for one turn, and the demarcation is
worth keeping straight: that essay argues the gavel **was seized**; this one asks why it is now being **gripped harder**.

[^sheep]: Matthew 25:37 — *"Lord, when saw we Thee an hungred, and fed Thee?"*

[^turn]: Ezekiel 33:11, KJV. The prophetic call is consistently to **turn** — the Hebrew *shuv*, to
turn or to return — rather than to feel, and that is the claim the body makes. It is a claim about
the **call**, not about the Greek: the relationship between *shuv* and *metanoeō* across the
Septuagint is not one-to-one (*shuv* is more often rendered *epistrephō*), and nothing here rests on
a lexical equation or on the derivation of *metanoia*, which would be a claim about what the word was
modeled on rather than about what it is used to mean. The verses either side sharpen rather than
soften it: 33:10 is the people saying *"if our transgressions and our sins be upon us, and we pine
away in them, how should we then live?"* — despair over guilt — and 33:11 answers that despair not
with a demand for more of it but with *turn*; and 33:12 says the day of the turn, not the record
before it, is what counts.

[^prodigal]: Luke 15:17–20, KJV — *"And when he came to himself…"*, then *"I will arise and go to my
father"*, then *"And he arose, and came to his father."* The parable is walked at length in [*The Way
Home Is Down*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-way-home-is-down) and its elder brother belongs to
[*Nothing to Get*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/nothing-to-get); one clause is taken here. One thing to say plainly, because a reader checking the verses will
find it: the son does confess — *"I have sinned against heaven, and before thee"* — and he rehearses
it at 15:18–19 and says it at 15:21. **The body's claim is about the order.** What comes first, at
15:17, is *he came to himself*: a realization, not a feeling, and the confession is the content of a
return already decided on, not the thing that decided it. The order is the argument, and the
confession is disclosed here rather than hidden.

[^prayer]: Matthew 6:10.

[^sow]: Galatians 6:7. The reading of the verse as terrain rather than as retaliation is [*The Highest Peak*](https://elmuffin.substack.com/p/the-highest-peak)'s, §VII.

[^job]: Job 42:7. The house convention capitalizes the deity pronoun (*of Me*); the King James
lowercases it.

[^neither]: John 9:2–3. The remainder of the answer — *but that the works of God should be made
manifest in him* — is a live translation question, since the Greek carries no punctuation and where
the sentence divides changes whether the blindness is given a purpose at all. The argument here
rests on the first word and only on it.
